# ABS failure modes seen in field data

Original reference notes describing how each failure mode in the project taxonomy
presents in owner narratives and why it matters for safety.

## Warning lamp illumination
The most common narrative is that the ABS lamp, often together with the brake, traction control or stability control lamps, came on and stayed on. On its own this indicates that the system detected a fault and disabled itself. The safety relevance depends on what failed and whether base braking is affected. A sustained rise in lamp complaints for one vehicle family is still valuable because it is usually the earliest and highest volume symptom of a hardware problem.

## Module, pump and hydraulic control unit failure
Owners report a failed ABS module, pump motor or hydraulic control unit, frequently after a dealer diagnosis. Internal causes include failed solder joints, worn pump motor brushes, sticking solenoid valves and internal brake fluid leaks. Because the module is expensive and often model specific, these narratives frequently mention high repair quotes and long waits for parts.

## Wheel speed sensor and tone ring faults
A sensor that reads low or intermittently makes the controller believe a wheel is locking. Typical causes are corrosion that pushes the sensor away from the tone ring, cracked or rusted tone rings, bearing play and chafed wiring. The usual symptoms are unwanted ABS activation at walking pace, a longer pedal travel just before the vehicle stops, and warning lamps.

## Unintended activation and pedal pulsation
ABS activation when no wheel is near lock lengthens low speed stopping distance because the controller releases pressure the driver needs. Narratives describe grinding, buzzing or pulsing in the pedal when stopping at an intersection or in a car park, sometimes with the vehicle rolling further than expected.

## Loss of braking or extended stopping distance
Narratives that describe a pedal sinking to the floor, a hard pedal, or the vehicle failing to slow deserve priority review whatever the suspected cause. Possible ABS related mechanisms include a valve stuck open inside the hydraulic unit, air or fluid loss through the unit, and software that releases pressure incorrectly. These narratives also arise from causes unrelated to ABS, so they need careful reading.

## Thermal events
A hydraulic unit that stays electrically powered when the vehicle is off can short circuit internally if brake fluid or moisture reaches the circuit board. The result can be an engine compartment fire while the vehicle is parked and unattended. Several large recall campaigns between 2020 and 2023 addressed exactly this mechanism by adding a lower rated fuse or replacing the module. Fire narratives that mention the ABS module, a burning smell near the brake unit, or a parked vehicle should be escalated.

## Electrical and software causes
Corroded connectors, damaged harnesses, blown fuses, low system voltage and calibration errors all produce ABS faults. Software defects matter increasingly because a calibration error can disable ABS and stability control across an entire model year at once, and because the remedy may be delivered over the air.

## Remedy availability
A recall or repair is only effective once parts exist. Narratives that report a back ordered module or an open recall with no remedy available indicate that vehicles with a known fault remain in service, which is itself a risk signal and a reason to track complaint volume after a recall is announced.
