# Regulatory context for brake defect surveillance in the United States

Original summary notes. They orient an analyst and are not legal advice. Always
read the current text of the regulation before relying on a requirement.

## Safety defects and the recall obligation
Under the National Traffic and Motor Vehicle Safety Act a manufacturer must conduct a recall when a vehicle or item of equipment contains a defect related to motor vehicle safety or does not comply with a Federal Motor Vehicle Safety Standard. Title 49 of the Code of Federal Regulations Part 573 requires the manufacturer to file a defect and noncompliance report with NHTSA within five working days of making that determination, and to follow it with quarterly reports on remedy completion.

## Early warning reporting and manufacturer communications
The TREAD Act of 2000 created the early warning reporting regime in 49 CFR Part 579. Larger manufacturers periodically report counts of consumer complaints, warranty claims, field reports, property damage claims, and claims involving death or injury. Manufacturers must also send NHTSA copies of communications issued to dealers and owners about defects, such as technical service bulletins. NHTSA publishes these manufacturer communications, and they often describe a fault and its repair well before any recall.

## Owner complaints and the investigation process
NHTSA collects vehicle owner questionnaires through its website, mobile application and hotline. The Office of Defects Investigation screens complaints together with other evidence. If a possible defect trend is found it can open a preliminary evaluation, which may be upgraded to an engineering analysis and may end in a recall request. A complaint is an unverified allegation by an owner. Complaint counts show reporting behaviour as well as underlying failure rates.

## FMVSS No. 135 light vehicle brake systems
FMVSS No. 135, at 49 CFR 571.135, sets service brake and parking brake performance requirements for passenger cars and for light multipurpose vehicles, trucks and buses. It includes stopping distance tests and requires brake system warning indicators, including an indicator that tells the driver about a malfunction of the antilock system where one is fitted.

## FMVSS No. 105 hydraulic and electric brake systems
FMVSS No. 105, at 49 CFR 571.105, covers hydraulic and electric brake systems on heavier vehicles that fall outside FMVSS No. 135. Recall reports for medium duty trucks sometimes cite it when an antilock malfunction indicator lamp does not light as required.

## FMVSS No. 121 air brake systems
FMVSS No. 121, at 49 CFR 571.121, covers trucks, buses and trailers with air brakes. It requires antilock braking on these vehicles and requires malfunction indicator lamps so the driver can see an ABS fault on the tractor or the trailer. Noncompliance recalls for heavy vehicles often concern the behaviour of that lamp.

## FMVSS No. 126 electronic stability control
FMVSS No. 126, at 49 CFR 571.126, requires electronic stability control on light vehicles and has applied to all new light vehicles since the 2012 model year. Stability control depends on the ABS sensors, valves and pump, so in practice every modern light vehicle carries ABS hardware, and an ABS module fault normally disables stability control as well.
